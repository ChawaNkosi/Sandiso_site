Manifesto

Building a Stronger, Inclusive Law Faculty

My manifesto outlines three practical initiatives that I will champion as Law Students' Council President. Together, they aim to make legal education more accessible, prepare students for diverse career opportunities, and create spaces where every student can participate, contribute and be heard.

  • Kramer Student Fund

    A student-led fund ensuring that no UCT Law student is forced to leave university because of financial hardship.

    Education should never be determined by financial circumstances. The Kramer Student Fund will be a student-led initiative dedicated to supporting UCT Law students facing financial hardship.

    Our Goal

    • Raise R500,000 during 2026–2027.
    • Provide financial assistance to deserving UCT Law students.
    • Ensure students are not forced to leave university because of debt.

    How we'll raise the funds

    • Community runs and marathons
    • Partnerships with churches and student organisations
    • Social media fundraising campaigns
    • Student-led fundraising initiatives

  • Alternative Career Day

    Expanding career opportunities by exposing students to the many paths available with an LLB beyond traditional legal practice.

    An LLB opens far more doors than becoming an attorney or advocate. Alternative Career Day will introduce students to employers and career paths they may never have considered.

    Organisations we'll invite

    • Special Investigation Unit (SIU)
    • Department of Correctional Services
    • SECTION27
    • Major South African banks

    The Goal

    To ensure that every UCT Law student graduates knowing the full range of career opportunities available and is better prepared for employment.

  • Inkulumo Yabafundi

    A forum where students, academics and legal professionals come together to discuss law beyond the classroom.

    Law should not exist only inside lecture halls. Inkulumo Yabafundi will create a welcoming space where students, lecturers and legal professionals can engage in meaningful discussions about the law and its impact on society.

    The initiative will

    • Host discussions on important legal judgments.
    • Bridge the gap between legal theory and practice.
    • Partner with SHAWCO Law and SLSJ.
    • Invite high school learners onto campus to experience legal education.
    • Create an environment where students can speak openly without fear of judgement.
